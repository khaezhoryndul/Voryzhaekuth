# Voryzhaekuth

> A silver oar rows the moon through a sea of unspoken names.

## The Boat on the Rooftop

The boat rests on the roof of a house that has no stairs. Its hull is dark wood, polished smooth by water that nobody has seen. A silver oar lies across the seats. Each morning, somebody finds a wet footprint beside the chimney, although the boat itself remains dry.

The people below call the house abandoned. They buy bread from its kitchen window every Thursday and leave payment in a blue bowl. The bowl contains a little seawater. Coins placed inside it sink farther than its depth permits.

Once a child dropped a button into the bowl. That evening, the boat acquired a sail. Its cloth was the same color as the child's missing coat, a coat that had disappeared many years before the child was born.

## A Passenger Made of Rain

The first passenger arrived during a drought. She stood in the street beneath a rain that fell on nobody else. Her face could be seen clearly until she turned toward the house; then it became the reflection of a window in moving water.

She carried a parcel tied with reeds. The baker, who had never been seen outside the kitchen, reached through the window and accepted it. His sleeve was covered in small scales. They caught the afternoon light and briefly showed the boat sailing beneath an unfamiliar moon.

The woman entered through the window without climbing. The rain stopped before touching the sill. Above the house, the silver oar moved once, and every hanging lamp in the street leaned toward the sea.

There was no sea nearby. The people checked their maps and found the coast exactly where it had always been. Only the names of its ports had changed.

## What the Oar Remembers

Along the oar's shaft are nine shallow grooves. Each contains a little darkness that remains visible at noon. An old fisherman claimed these grooves recorded journeys, but refused to say whether he had made them or survived them.

He asked to see the oar. The baker gave him a loaf with a silver crust. Inside lay a small wooden bridge, complete with railings, a lantern, and a figure waiting halfway across.

The fisherman recognized the lantern. He set the loaf on the pavement and knelt beside it. The figure on the bridge turned its head. From the roof came the sound of something enormous drawing a breath through water.

When the fisherman stood, his beard was wet. He left his boots beside the blue bowl and walked away barefoot. Nobody followed him beyond the place where his footprints stopped pointing in the direction he was walking.

## The Animal Beneath the House

At low tide, which the house observes without consulting the coast, its foundations become transparent. Beneath them lies a creature with a crown of pale horns. Its body continues under the street and beyond the edges of the town.

The people have learned to recognize its movements. A shifting claw makes the wells taste of pears. The folding of a wing causes every door to open onto the wrong side of its threshold. When its eye moves beneath the kitchen floor, the bread is baked with an empty space inside each loaf.

The baker calls this space the passenger's place. He asks customers to leave it untouched. Some obey. Others fill it with butter and dream of rowing for the rest of the night.

Those dreams have a common shore: black sand, a white tree, and a blue bowl large enough to contain a house.

## Words Found on a Reed

The parcel's reeds were eventually discovered in the chimney. They had grown into a narrow ladder. On its lowest rung, someone had scratched four groups of letters:

*Vor — yzhae — kuth — imbra.*

The child climbed the ladder in a dream and returned with a second button. This one was made of silver. When held beside the first, it produced a faint sound like two people speaking through a wall.

The baker placed both buttons in the bowl. The water rose until it stood above the rim without spilling. Inside it appeared the woman made of rain, seated in the boat with the parcel on her knees.

She untied the reeds. There was nothing inside them except a small shadow shaped like an oar. She lifted it, and the moon outside the kitchen window moved a little to the left.

## A Shore Without Arrival

The boat left the roof on a windless evening. Nobody saw it rise. One moment it rested beside the chimney; the next, its reflection was crossing the sky while the boat itself remained invisible.

The house still sells bread on Thursdays. The blue bowl still accepts payment. Sometimes a coin returns with a hole in its center, and through that hole a customer can see black sand beneath a white tree.

The child has grown old enough to forget the coat. The buttons have never been returned. On certain nights, a silver line crosses the moon, pauses, and continues beyond the stars.

There is still a wet footprint beside the chimney.

The next passenger has not yet learned their name.
